## 1. a-ha – Take On Me (1985) — 80s
- **Video ID:** `djV11Xbc914` ✅ (oembed: "a-ha - Take On Me (Official Video) [4K]")
- **Channel:** a-ha (official)
- **Video:** Girl reading a comic is pulled into its pages; pencil-sketch rotoscope animation collides with live action as the band dodges cartoon villains — restored in 4K.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Started life as an instrumental called "The Juicy Fruit Song," written by Magne Furuholmen and Pål Waaktaar-Savoy in their earlier rock band Bridges — years before Morten Harket joined. (Mental Floss)
  2. [intro] Released three times before anyone cared: the 1984 Tony Mansfield version flopped in the UK, the 1985 Alan Tarney re-record flopped too — until the new video changed everything. (Wikipedia)
  3. [early] The pencil-sketch look is rotoscoping: live footage traced frame by frame. Animators Michael Patterson and Candace Reckinger hand-drew ~3,000 frames — 16 weeks of animation work. (Deadline)
  4. [early] Directed by Steve Barron — the same man behind Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean" — who won Best Direction for this at the 1986 MTV VMAs. (Radio X)
  5. [mid] Harket's vocal spans 2.5+ octaves, soaring to falsetto at the end of the chorus — Rolling Stone called it one of the hardest-to-sing choruses in pop. (Wikipedia/Rolling Stone)
  6. [mid] The two villains were played by Philip Jackson (later Inspector Japp in *Poirot*) and Alfie Curtis (Dr. Evazan in *Star Wars*). (Warped Factor)
  7. [mid] The girl in the video was Bunty Bailey, Harket's real-life girlfriend at the time — the comic-book romance was basically a true love story. (Warped Factor)
  8. [late] Hit No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 on October 19, 1985, in its 15th chart week — the first Norwegian group ever to top the US chart. (Wikipedia)
  9. [late] In the UK it sat at No. 2 for three weeks, blocked by Jennifer Rush's "The Power of Love" — that year's biggest UK single. (Wikipedia)
  10. [outro] In September 2024 it became the first 1980s music video to reach 2 billion YouTube views — 40 years after recording. (Radio X)

## 2. Michael Jackson – Thriller (1982) — 80s
- **Video ID:** `sOnqjkJTMaA` ✅ (oembed: "Michael Jackson - Thriller (Official 4K Video)")
- **Channel:** michaeljacksonVEVO
- **Release:** 14-min short film premiered Dec 2, 1983; song from the 1982 album, single Jan 23, 1984
- **Video:** A date-night movie turns into a werewolf nightmare, a graveyard zombie dance, and the most imitated music video ever made.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Songwriter Rod Temperton's first demo was called "Starlight." Quincy Jones wanted a better album title, so Temperton brainstormed hundreds of options — "Midnight Man" — until he woke up with "Thriller" stuck in his head. (The Telegraph/iHeart; Bassculture)
  2. [intro] Horror legend Vincent Price recorded his closing narration in just two takes. Temperton wrote Price's script in the back of a taxi on the way to the studio. (Fandom album wiki; popredux80)
  3. [early] Michael hired director John Landis after seeing *An American Werewolf in London* (1981) — he specifically wanted transformation scenes like Landis's. (Wikipedia; Virgin Radio)
  4. [early] Shot on 35mm film with feature-film production values — Landis wanted a theatrical short that would bring short films back to cinemas. (Wikipedia)
  5. [mid] Final cost ~$500,000 — roughly 10× the average early-'80s video budget. To pay for it, they sold a behind-the-scenes documentary to MTV and Showtime for ~$250,000 each. (Wikipedia; Virgin Radio; Grunge)
  6. [mid] The iconic red jacket was designed by Deborah Nadoolman Landis — the director's wife and a professional costume designer. (Music Video Wiki)
  7. [mid] The zombie dance was choreographed by Michael Peters with Michael himself — now one of the most copied routines in history, recreated every Halloween. (Music Video Wiki)
  8. [late] Co-star Ola Ray was a former Playboy Playmate, spotted for the role through her modeling work. (mjjcommunity; Music Video Wiki)
  9. [late] MTV debuted it December 2, 1983, running it 3–5 times a day and advertising the next airing after every play. Ratings ran ~10× the network's normal audience; the album spiked to a million copies a week. (iHeart/Vanity Fair; Warped Factor)
  10. [outro] In 2009 "Thriller" became the first music video ever preserved in the Library of Congress National Film Registry. (VEVO description; Music Video Wiki)

## 3. Cyndi Lauper – Girls Just Want to Have Fun (1983) — 80s
- **Video ID:** `PIb6AZdTr-A` ✅ (oembed: "Cyndi Lauper - Girls Just Want To Have Fun (Official Video)")
- **Channel:** CyndiLauperVEVO
- **Video:** Cyndi sneaks out past her grumpy dad and dances through Manhattan's Lower East Side with a joyful crowd, ending in a giant bedroom house party.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Not Lauper's creation — Philadelphia musician Robert Hazard wrote and demoed it in 1979 from a man's point of view. Lauper refused to sing it as written and reworked the lyrics into a girl-power anthem. (Wikipedia)
  2. [early] Lauper was so unsure of the track she cut demo after demo in different styles — nothing clicked until she leaned into a bouncy, summer-vacation-commercial sound in new keys. (Deseret News/"Let The Canary Sing" coverage)
  3. [early] Her very first solo single, released September 6, 1983, cut at New York's Record Plant two months earlier — the breakout that made a former bar-band singer a global star overnight. (Wikipedia)
  4. [mid] Shot on the Lower East Side in summer 1983 on a shoestring — under $35,000, volunteer cast, borrowed gear. MTV put it in heavy rotation back when the channel played videos around the clock. (Wikipedia/FilmAffinity)
  5. [mid] Dad casting was perfect '80s weirdness: wrestling legend "Captain" Lou Albano plays her disapproving father; her real-life mother Catrine plays mom. Also appearing: her attorney, manager, brother Butch, and dog Sparkle. (Wikipedia/Radio X)
  6. [mid] Lauper deliberately cast women of every background dancing together so every little girl watching could see herself on screen. Decades later, protesters carried signs reading "Girls Just Want to Have Fun-damental Rights." (American Songwriter/Radio X)
  7. [late] Peaked at #2 on the Billboard Hot 100 in March 1984 — blocked by Van Halen's "Jump." Still spent 25 straight weeks on the chart and hit #1 in Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. (Wikipedia)
  8. [late] Made history at the first-ever MTV VMAs in 1984, winning the first Best Female Video award — and nominated for Video of the Year the same night. (Wikipedia)
  9. [late] Earned two Grammy noms at the 1985 ceremony (Record of the Year, Best Female Pop Vocal); that night she won Best New Artist and the album took Best Album Package. (Wikipedia)
  10. [outro] Parent album *She's So Unusual* was the first debut by a female artist to land four top-five Hot 100 hits. The video crossed one billion YouTube views in January 2022. (Wikipedia)

## 4. Wham! – Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go (1984) — 80s
- **Video ID:** `ELflyACZXQQ` ✅ (oembed: "Wham! - Wake Me Up Before You Go Go (Official HD Video)")
- **Channel:** WhamVEVO
- **Video:** George Michael and Andrew Ridgeley perform the bubblegum anthem for a screaming teen crowd at London's Brixton Academy in the iconic oversized "CHOOSE LIFE" T-shirts.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] The title came from a sleepy note Andrew Ridgeley scribbled for his parents — he meant "wake me up before you go" but doubled "up," then doubled "go" to cover the mistake. He still keeps the original note, reproduced in his memoir. (Wikipedia; The Current)
  2. [intro] George Michael wrote, produced, and arranged the whole track himself, recording with a live band in two days at Sarm West Studio 2. He claims a single take with no overdubs — and the drum machine stayed in because the real drummer was late. (Wikipedia)
  3. [early] Released in the UK May 14, 1984, it debuted at #4 and hit #1 seven days later, holding two weeks — Wham!'s first UK chart-topper. (Wikipedia)
  4. [early] Repeated the trick in the US: three weeks at #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in November 1984 — their first American #1. (Wikipedia)
  5. [mid] Directed by Andy Morahan and filmed at Brixton Academy in South London, the duo playing to mostly teenage extras. (Wikipedia)
  6. [mid] The "CHOOSE LIFE" shirts were Katharine Hamnett's bold anti-war, anti-drug design — they kicked off a massive '80s fashion craze big enough for its own segment on VH1's *I Love the 80s*. (Wikipedia; Frieze)
  7. [mid] Blink and you'll miss it: the video ends with a cheesy on-screen ad — "GO-GO BUY IT! thank you" — on the final black frame. (Wikipedia)
  8. [late] George Michael was never that fond of it — he told Rolling Stone it was "undoubtedly the most remembered Wham! song because it is that much more stupid than anything else." (Parade)
  9. [late] VH1 ranked it #28 on its "100 Greatest Songs of the '80s." (Wikipedia)
  10. [outro] In *Zoolander* (2001) it soundtracks the gasoline fight scene — but only as a temp placeholder. Sum 41, hired to write an original song, saw the rough cut and insisted the Wham! track stay. (iHeart; Cracked)

- **Video ID:** `c6rP-YP4c5I` ✅ (oembed: "Shakira - Try Everything (Official Video)")
- **Channel:** shakiraVEVO
- **Video:** Intercuts studio footage of Shakira performing with scenes from Disney's *Zootopia*.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Shakira's first-ever animated-film voice role: she plays Gazelle, Zootopia's pop superstar, who performs this song at the movie's climactic concert. (Wikipedia)
  2. [intro] The video is half studio session, half movie magic — Shakira in headphones belting into a mic, cutting back and forth with *Zootopia* clips. (Wikipedia; ABC News)
  3. [early] Sia co-wrote it with Norwegian hitmakers Stargate — and years before Shakira, girl group Fifth Harmony cut a version in 2013 that never got released. (Wikipedia)
  4. [early] Shakira's first single released under Walt Disney Records, kicking off the *Zootopia* soundtrack on January 26, 2016. (Wikipedia)
  5. [mid] Earned a 2017 Grammy nomination for Best Song Written for Visual Media — the category for songs made for movies and TV. (Wikipedia/Grammy Awards)
  6. [mid] Debuted on the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 63 after the video dropped — 33,000 downloads and ~3 million US streams in its first tracking week. (Wikipedia; Billboard)
  7. [mid] A genuine global hit: No. 1 in Lebanon and Japan's international chart, and 4× platinum in the US (over 4 million units). (Wikipedia; RIAA)
  8. [late] The movie was no slouch: *Zootopia* crossed $1 billion worldwide in June 2016 — only the fourth animated film ever to do so. (Disney; Wikipedia)
  9. [late] Kids worldwide got their own version: re-recorded in Mandarin, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Polish, and Ukrainian so Gazelle could sing in each country's dub. (Wikipedia)
  10. [outro] Billboard named it one of the 12 best Disney songs of the 21st century in 2020, and it later landed at No. 19 on their all-time greatest Disney songs chart. (Billboard)

## 6. Jimmy Buffett – Margaritaville (1977) — 70s
- **Video ID:** `mrF4nF8VUb4` ✅ (oembed: "Jimmy Buffett - Margaritaville (Audio)")
- **Channel:** JimmyBuffettVEVO
- **Sourcing: option (b) — official AUDIO, not a music video.** No official narrative music video exists on his channel; the verified upload is the official VEVO audio. No fallback needed. (Note for the site: facts should be spaced evenly over the static-audio upload.)
- **Upload:** Official audio track for "Margaritaville."
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Buffett started writing it in Austin, Texas, right after a burrito and his first-ever margarita at a restaurant called Lung's Cocina del Sur — finishing the lyrics that same night on a friend's deck, then polishing it in Key West. (American Songwriter/Wide Open Country)
  2. [intro] His working title wasn't "Margaritaville" — he first wanted "Wasting Away in Austin, Texasville," then decided the cocktail deserved the headline. (American Songwriter)
  3. [early] When Buffett told producer Norbert Putnam he wanted to write about losing his flip-flop and salt shaker, Putnam said: "That's a terrible idea for a song." Buffett returned days later with the finished tune, and Putnam admitted it wasn't a song — "it was a movie." (Sound on Sound/Holler Country)
  4. [early] Putnam had produced Elvis, but Buffett made him leave Nashville for Criteria Studios in Miami — reasoning: if you're singing about beach life, you record near the water. (American Songwriter)
  5. [early] Buffett's biggest solo hit ever: #8 Billboard Hot 100, #1 Easy Listening, #13 country. Billboard ranked it the 14th biggest pop single of all 1977. (Billboard/Wikipedia)
  6. [mid] The "lost shaker of salt" line was literal — the idea started after a Key West night where he lost a flip-flop, stepped on a pop-top can tab, and couldn't find the salt for his margarita. (Sound on Sound)
  7. [mid] In July 1977 it sat in the Top 10 alongside Streisand, Manilow, Frampton, Shaun Cassidy, and Andy Gibb — and it's the only one from that week's Top 10 now in both the Grammy Hall of Fame AND the National Recording Registry. (Billboard)
  8. [mid] Buffett was thrilled it was a hit because it finally let him buy his own boat. It grew into a multibillion-dollar empire: the first Margaritaville restaurant (Key West, 1985), resorts, a Broadway musical, a SiriusXM channel, his own blenders. (American Songwriter/Parade)
  9. [late] In 2023 the song entered the Library of Congress National Recording Registry for cultural importance. Buffett said he'd been lucky to "put his thumb on the pulse of something that people can connect with." (Library of Congress)
  10. [outro] After Buffett's passing on September 1, 2023, it stormed back onto the Hot 100 at #38 — first appearance in 46 years. Fans streamed his catalog 78.6 million times that week, up nearly 1,500%. (Billboard)

## 7. Queen – Bohemian Rhapsody (1975) — 70s
- **Video ID:** `fJ9rUzIMcZQ` ✅ (oembed: "Queen – Bohemian Rhapsody (Official Video Remastered)")
- **Channel:** Queen Official
- **Video:** The band lip-syncs the epic in a dark studio, opening with the famous diamond formation of glowing faces — the official HD remaster.
- **Facts:**
  1. [intro] Freddie Mercury wrote the whole thing — a "mock opera" stitched from three separate songs he'd written, fully mapped out in his head before the band hit the studio. (Wikipedia)
  2. [intro] At nearly six minutes, label EMI said it was far too long for radio and pushed to cut it. Mercury refused — then his friend, DJ Kenny Everett, got a tape with a wink and a "promise never to play it," and played it 14 times in one weekend instead. (Parade/Wikipedia)
  3. [early] Recording took three weeks (Aug 24–Sep 1975) across five studios. With only 24-track tape, layers were "bounced down" so many times the final mix sat on eighth-generation tape. (Wikipedia)
  4. [early] The opera section alone needed ~180 vocal overdubs — Mercury, Brian May, and Roger Taylor sang 10–12 hours a day until the harmonies stacked into a giant fake choir. (Wikipedia)
  5. [mid] The most expensive single ever made at the time — all that studio-hopping and hundreds of overdubs nearly bankrupted the band. (BBC/Best Classic Bands)
  6. [mid] The video was shot in under four hours at Elstree Studios on November 10, 1975, for ~£4,500 — because the band was touring and couldn't mime it on Top of the Pops. The diamond face pose recreates the Queen II album cover by Mick Rock. (Louder/Gold Radio/brianmay.com)
  7. [late] Chart monster: nine weeks at UK #1 in 1975, then #1 again for five weeks after Mercury's death in 1991 — the first song to top the UK charts twice with the same recording. US: #9 in 1976, #2 in 1992 after *Wayne's World*. (Wikipedia)
  8. [late] After the 2018 biopic it became the most-streamed song of the 20th century; in 2022 the Library of Congress added it to the National Recording Registry. (Wikipedia)
  9. [outro] A 2023 auction of Mercury's belongings revealed his handwritten draft — first titled "Mongolian Rhapsody," with "Mongolian" crossed out and "Bohemian" written in. (Wikipedia)
  10. [outro] Rolling Stone ranked it #17 on its 2021 "500 Greatest Songs"; readers voted Mercury's vocal the greatest in rock history; it entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2004. (Wikipedia)
